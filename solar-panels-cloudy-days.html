Do Solar Panels Still Work On Cloudy Days?

Solar panels work on cloudy days. They generate electricity from diffuse light (the scattered daylight that passes through cloud cover), not only from direct sunshine. On a heavily overcast day, output typically falls to 10 to 25 percent of a panel's peak rated capacity. In the South East of England, where annual solar irradiance is around 1,100 kilowatt-hours per square metre, a 4 kWp south-facing system generates approximately 3,400 to 3,600 kWh per year across all weather conditions combined.

If you have spent any time researching solar panels for your home in West Sussex, somebody has probably told you not to worry about the cloud cover. What they have not told you is the figure that actually answers the question. Not what happens on a grey Tuesday in November. The annual generation total for a south-facing system in your area. That annual total determines your savings, your payback period, and whether solar makes financial sense for your property.

This article explains how solar panels handle cloudy conditions, what West Sussex's generation profile actually looks like month by month, and why the cloudy-day question is really an argument for battery storage rather than against solar. How solar panels generate power from daylight, not just sunshine

Solar panels generate electricity through the photovoltaic effect. how solar panels convert sunlight into electricity is covered in more detail in our main explainer. The relevant point for cloudy-day performance is this: photovoltaic cells respond to light, not heat. They capture photons from the visible light spectrum and the wavelengths that sit just beyond it. Both pass through cloud cover to varying degrees depending on cloud thickness.

That is why a heavily overcast sky does not cut generation to zero. The light is scattered and diffused rather than blocked entirely. Your panels capture what gets through. As Energy Saving Trust's advice on solar panels confirms: the cells do not need direct sunlight to work, and output tracks the intensity of the available light rather than the presence of blue sky.

We see this in monitoring dashboards throughout the year. A typical West Sussex autumn day with partial cloud and some breaks will show generation running at 40 to 70 percent of the summer peak for the same time of day. The curve drops, it does not flatline. What changes is how much of your generation you use directly versus how much comes from the battery or the grid.

What happens to output when cloud comes in over West Sussex

Cloud cover reduces generation in proportion to how much light it blocks. Thin high cloud cuts less than thick low cloud. A bright overcast day (the kind where you need sunglasses but cannot see the sun directly) typically runs at 60 to 80 percent of clear-sky output. Heavy, dark cloud before rain brings it down to the 10 to 25 percent range. That is the honest figure, and it is worth knowing.

The edge-of-cloud effect

When sunlight catches the edge of a cloud at the right angle, it focuses rather than scatters. The result is a brief spike in panel output, sometimes above the system's rated peak capacity for a few seconds. Monitoring systems log these as sharp upward spikes. It is a real effect, documented in PV performance data, and it is one reason why partly cloudy days can sometimes outperform clear ones over a short window.

What this range tells you: on most overcast days, your panels are generating something useful. The question is whether that something is enough to cover your household's daytime consumption on its own, or whether you are supplementing from stored battery charge or the grid. That is a design question, not a weather question. We will tell you honestly that a sustained grey November week will knock your generation down. That is real, and it is worth planning for. It is exactly why battery storage is part of the standard system design for most West Sussex households we install for.

One more thing worth knowing: rain is not the enemy of solar panels. Energy Saving Trust's guide to common solar myths notes that panels absorb wavelengths that pass through clouds, and in practice a good rainstorm cleans the panel surface of dust and debris that would otherwise reduce efficiency. Generation on a rainy day follows cloud density, but the clean panel surface that follows often improves output for the days after.

Battery storage and the cloudy-day question

If your concern about cloudy days is about what happens to your energy supply when the panels are not generating much, battery storage is the practical answer. A battery charged from surplus generation on sunny spring and summer days covers the gap during lower-generating periods. On a grey November day when your panels are running at 20 percent, the battery fills the difference rather than the grid.

The typical West Sussex household generates far more than it can use during daylight hours in the spring and summer. Without a battery, that surplus is exported to the grid at the Smart Export Guarantee rate. This is useful income, but lower per unit than what you pay for grid electricity. A battery captures that surplus and uses it in the evening, overnight, and on the lower-generating days of winter. Self-consumption rates for a solar-only system are typically 20 to 30 percent. Add a well-sized battery and that figure moves to 60 to 70 percent or higher, depending on the household's usage pattern and whether they are charging an EV at home.

Do solar panels work in winter in the UK?

Yes, solar panels generate electricity in winter, but at reduced output compared to summer. Shorter daylight hours and a lower sun angle mean the system generates less per day from November through January. For a 4 kWp south-facing system in West Sussex, expect 80 to 150 kWh per month in the two lowest-producing months. The annual total is still around 3,400 to 3,600 kWh, with the majority generated between March and September. Battery storage helps use more of the spring and summer surplus to offset the winter shortfall.

How much do solar panels generate on a rainy day?

Generation on a rainy day follows cloud density rather than the rain itself. Heavy cloud (the kind that comes with persistent rain) typically brings output to 10 to 25 percent of peak rated capacity. Light or intermittent rain with partial cloud produces higher output, often 40 to 60 percent of peak. Panels are designed to operate safely in wet weather. Rain also washes dust and debris from the panel surface, which can improve efficiency in the days that follow.

Is it worth getting solar panels in West Sussex if the weather is often cloudy?

Yes. West Sussex sits in one of the UK's highest-irradiance zones, receiving around 1,100 kWh of solar irradiance per square metre per year. That is 15 to 20 percent more than northern England. Panels generate electricity across the full year, including on cloudy days. The financial case is driven by the annual generation total of around 3,400 to 3,600 kWh for a 4 kWp south-facing system, not by individual overcast days. The combination of bill savings and Smart Export Guarantee income makes West Sussex a strong location for solar investment.

How does battery storage help on cloudy days?

A battery stores surplus electricity generated on high-output days and uses it when generation is lower, such as on overcast days or in the evening. Rather than drawing from the grid when cloud reduces panel output, your household draws from the battery instead. A well-sized battery charged from spring and summer generation can cover evening and overnight demand even in lower-generating autumn months. This increases self-consumption (the proportion of your solar electricity you use directly) from around 20 to 30 percent without a battery to 60 to 70 percent or higher with one.

Do I need to clean my solar panels after cloudy or rainy weather?

Generally no. In the UK's climate, rain provides enough natural cleaning to maintain panel performance without intervention. Pollen and dust can build up during dry spring and summer periods, and a good rainstorm clears this effectively. If you are in a location with nearby road traffic, construction, or agriculture, periodic manual cleaning once or twice a year may improve output slightly. Most UK panel manufacturers do not require cleaning as a maintenance condition of the warranty. Check your specific panel documentation if in doubt.

What is the Smart Export Guarantee and does it still pay out in winter?

The Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) is a UK government scheme that requires licensed energy suppliers to pay households for surplus electricity exported to the grid. It pays out whenever your panels generate more than you are using, including in winter. Winter SEG income is lower than summer simply because panels generate less and households tend to use more, so there is less surplus to export. To qualify for SEG payments, your installer must be MCS-certified and the system must be registered. All installations we carry out are MCS-registered as part of our standard process.
