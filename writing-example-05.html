The Edexcel A Level Maths Textbook: Year 1, Year 2 and Combined — What Schools Should Order

Edexcel A Level Mathematics uses the Pearson 2017 specification (9MA0), which divides into Pure Mathematics and Statistics and Mechanics. Schools typically need four core Pearson textbooks: Pure Year 1, Pure Year 2, Statistics and Mechanics Year 1, and Statistics and Mechanics Year 2. A combined Year 1 and 2 edition from Oxford University Press is available for departments that prefer a single volume. No new specification is in place for 2026 or 2027.

If you are ordering Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks for a new cohort this September, the first question is always the same: Year 1, Year 2, combined, or all of the above? The answer depends on how your department teaches the course, how your budget is structured, and whether your students are sitting AS or the full A Level. We have been putting together these orders for secondary and sixth form departments, and the same questions come up every time. This guide walks through the Pearson textbook range, explains the logic behind each option, and covers what to send us when you are ready to place the order.

The Edexcel A Level Maths specification in 2026: what is current

The live specification is the Pearson Edexcel Level 3 Advanced GCE in Mathematics (9MA0), introduced for first teaching in September 2017 and first examined in summer 2018. That specification is still current for 2026 and 2027. There is no replacement specification in place or announced.

The course divides into two strands. Pure Mathematics covers algebra, calculus, trigonometry, vectors, and proof. Statistics and Mechanics covers statistical distributions, hypothesis testing, kinematics, and forces. Both strands run across Year 1 and Year 2, and both are assessed at the end of the two-year programme through three papers.

One thing worth flagging: Pearson issued a minor wording update to the AS Level specification (8MA0) in October 2025, but this did not change the A Level (9MA0) content, structure, or approved textbooks. If your department co-teaches AS and A Level, the same textbook range covers both.

What this means for your order

The textbooks on your department's current list are almost certainly still the right ones. If you ordered Pearson Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks in 2022 or 2023, you do not need to replace them for a 2026 cohort. The specification has not changed.

The combined edition: who it suits and who it does not

Oxford University Press publishes an alternative: the Edexcel A Level Maths Year 1 and 2 Combined Student Book, endorsed for the Pearson Edexcel 2017 specification. It covers both years of the course in a single volume across Pure and Applied content. The ISBN is 9780198413158.

The appeal is straightforward: one book per student for the full two-year course. Some departments find that simpler to manage, and it reduces the number of budget lines.

But there are trade-offs worth knowing before you decide. The Pearson split books integrate directly with Pearson's own ActiveLearn digital platform and the interactive scheme of work on the Edexcel qualifications site. If your department uses those tools for planning and assessment, the split books work more naturally with them. The OUP combined edition is the better fit for departments that want a clean single-volume resource and are not running Pearson's digital platform centrally.

There is also a practical question about year groups. If some students in a teaching group are sitting AS Level and others are sitting the full A Level, the split books give you more flexibility. Year 1/AS students need only the two Year 1 titles. Full A Level students need all four. The combined edition does not offer that separation, and ordering it for a mixed group means paying for Year 2 content that AS students will not sit.

Our recommendation on format

For most secondary schools, the four Pearson split books are the right choice. They align with Pearson's digital tools, they give you flexibility for AS and A Level groups, and they are the reference point most Maths departments and tutors will be working from. The OUP combined edition makes sense if your whole cohort is sitting the full A Level and your department wants to keep the resource list simple.

CGP alternatives: the case for having both

CGP also publishes a student textbook range for Edexcel A Level Maths, with separate titles for Pure Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781789088397), Pure Year 2 (ISBN 9781789088410), Statistics and Mechanics Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781789088403), and Statistics and Mechanics Year 2 (ISBN 9781789088427). These are not replacements for the Pearson books in most departments. They work best as supplements: a more accessible presentation for students who find Pearson's worked examples harder to follow, or a bank of additional practice questions for independent study.

We see departments order both Pearson and CGP titles on the same list fairly regularly. There is no conflict. We consolidate both into a single school order, and the same discount structure applies across publishers.

Year 1 versus Year 2 versus combined: the ordering logic

The question of whether to order Year 1 books, Year 2 books, or both is straightforward once you know where your students are in the course.

Year 12 students starting in September need the Year 1/AS titles: Pure Year 1 and Statistics and Mechanics Year 1. That covers the full Year 12 Edexcel Maths content. Year 13 students moving into their second year need the Year 2 titles: Pure Year 2 and Statistics and Mechanics Year 2. If you are ordering for both year groups at the same time, you need all four Pearson titles.

We see one ordering pattern that causes problems. A department orders Year 1 books in September for a new Year 12 cohort, then comes back the following July for Year 2 books for the same students. That is the right approach on paper, but it means two separate orders, two separate quotes, and the risk that prices have moved between September and July. If your budget allows, ordering all four titles in one go for the incoming Year 12 cohort removes that second-order overhead. We can hold the Year 2 books to a delivery date that suits your Year 13 start, so your storeroom is not stacking books a year early.

Ordering for both year groups

If you are placing a whole-department order covering both Year 12 and Year 13, include all four Pearson titles plus any CGP additions on the same list. One list, one quote, one delivery. We will flag any stock availability issues before we confirm.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks do schools need for 2026?

The current Pearson Edexcel A Level Mathematics specification (9MA0) has not changed for 2026. Schools teaching the full A Level course need four Pearson student books: Pure Mathematics Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781292183398), Pure Mathematics Year 2 (ISBN 9781292183404), Statistics and Mechanics Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781292232539), and Statistics and Mechanics Year 2 (ISBN 9781446944073). These are the same titles that have been in use since first teaching began in September 2017. No replacement specification or new textbook range has been introduced.

What is the difference between the Year 1 and Year 2 Edexcel Maths textbooks?

The Year 1/AS Pearson textbooks cover the content taught in the first year of the A Level programme, which also matches the full AS Level Mathematics qualification. This includes the introductory Pure Mathematics topics and the Year 1 Statistics and Mechanics content. The Year 2 books continue from where Year 1 ends, covering the additional Pure, Statistics, and Mechanics content that appears in the A Level papers 1, 2, and 3. Year 12 students need the Year 1 books. Year 13 students need the Year 2 books. Schools placing a whole-cohort order typically order all four titles at once.

Is there a combined Edexcel A Level Maths textbook that covers both years?

Yes. Oxford University Press publishes the Edexcel A Level Maths Year 1 and 2 Combined Student Book (ISBN 9780198413158), which is endorsed by Pearson for use with the 9MA0 specification. It covers both the Pure and Applied content for Years 1 and 2 in a single volume. The main trade-off is that it does not separate out AS Level content for students sitting only the AS qualification, and it integrates less directly with Pearson's ActiveLearn digital platform than the split Pearson books do.

Do schools need to replace Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks for the 2026 or 2027 exams?

No. The Pearson Edexcel A Level Mathematics specification (9MA0) has been unchanged since its introduction for first teaching in September 2017. The textbooks published for that specification remain current and aligned with the 2026 and 2027 examinations. There is no new specification in place for those exam years, and no new textbook range has been introduced to replace the existing Pearson series. Departments that ordered Pearson Edexcel Maths student books in recent years do not need to replace them.

What is the difference between the Pearson and CGP Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks?

Pearson publishes the official Edexcel A Level Mathematics series, written to align directly with the 9MA0 specification and integrated with Pearson's own digital resources including ActiveLearn and the online SolutionBank. CGP publishes a separate student textbook range for the same specification: Pure Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781789088397), Pure Year 2 (ISBN 9781789088410), Statistics and Mechanics Year 1/AS (ISBN 9781789088403), and Statistics and Mechanics Year 2 (ISBN 9781789088427). Many departments use Pearson as the primary course text and CGP as a supplementary resource or alternative for students who prefer a different presentation style. Both ranges are current for the 2026 and 2027 examinations.

Can Edexcel A Level Maths textbooks be included in a whole-school or multi-subject order?

Yes, and this is where consolidation saves schools significant admin time. We supply across all major publishers, so your Maths textbooks from Pearson can sit on the same order as your Biology titles from Hodder, your English class readers, and your Geography resources from OUP. We consolidate across publishers into one quote, one invoice, and one delivery. For schools managing orders across multiple departments, this removes the overhead of placing separate orders with different publishers or suppliers.
