What survives when your solar installer closes

The single most important thing to understand: your solar panels do not need the installer to keep working. The system on your roof operates independently. There is no connection to maintain, no licence that lapses with the company, no software that stops running. Your panels will carry on generating electricity regardless of what happens to the business that fitted them.

What does survive installer closure is your manufacturer warranty. Panel manufacturers and inverter manufacturers issue their warranties directly. They are separate from the installer's guarantee, and they do not expire when the installation company dissolves. A panel warranty typically runs for 25 years and covers performance degradation. An inverter warranty typically runs between 5 and 12 years depending on the brand. Both of these remain valid as long as the equipment was registered correctly at the time of installation.

What does not survive is the installer's own workmanship warranty. That guarantee was issued by the company, and if the company has ceased trading, the company cannot honour it. There is one exception, and it matters: if your installation came with an Insurance Backed Guarantee, the IBG is specifically designed to survive exactly this situation. The IBG section below covers how to check whether you have one and what to do with it.

One other thing that can stop working: your monitoring portal login. If your original installer registered your inverter under their company account rather than your own, the login may have died with the business. The data is not lost: it is held on the manufacturer's servers. You will need to contact the inverter manufacturer with your serial number and proof of property address to transfer the monitoring account to your name. This is a separate process from the warranty and does not affect your system's performance while you sort it out.

Check your paperwork before anything else

Before you call anyone, find your installation paperwork. What you have, and what is in it, determines your options.

The most important document is your MCS certificate. If your system was installed by an MCS-certified installer, you should have received this within 10 working days of completion. Check the top right corner of the certificate. It will name the insurance provider and policy number if your installation was registered for an IBG. This is the fastest way to find out whether you have insurance-backed cover and who to claim from.

Your installation contract will tell you what workmanship warranty period was promised, and whether it referenced an IBG. Your product manuals should carry the manufacturer warranty details and, in some cases, a warranty registration card or reference number. If the equipment was not registered with the manufacturer at the time of installation, contact the manufacturer now with your serial number. Some manufacturers will still validate a late registration; others will not, so this is worth doing promptly.

If you cannot find your MCS certificate, it is not gone. MCS holds records of every certified installation since 2010, and because your installer is no longer trading, you can request a copy directly from MCS. Call the MCS Customer Support Helpdesk on 0333 103 8130 with your full property address and proof that you live there. There is a £36 admin fee for a certificate copy, and MCS will issue it within 10 working days.

If your monitoring portal login no longer works

Contact the inverter manufacturer directly with your inverter serial number and proof that you own the property. They will transfer the system from the installer's account to your own homeowner account. This is separate from your warranty. Your warranty is unaffected by a monitoring login issue. Common inverter brands, including GivEnergy, SolarEdge, SMA, and Solis, all offer homeowner account transfers. The process usually takes a few working days.

The Insurance Backed Guarantee: what it covers and how to claim

An Insurance Backed Guarantee is an insurance policy that sits behind the installer's workmanship warranty. If the installer ceases trading and can no longer honour that warranty, the IBG insurer steps in instead. The whole point of the IBG is to survive exactly the situation you are in now.

IBGs are a mandatory requirement for MCS-certified installers and for members of the Renewable Energy Consumer Code (RECC). The RECC's consumer protection guidance on deposit and workmanship warranty protection sets out the requirement clearly: all RECC members must insure their workmanship warranties and customer deposits against the risk of the company ceasing to trade. If your installation was carried out by an MCS-certified or RECC-registered installer, an IBG should have been issued.

IBG cover typically runs for 2, 5, or 10 years from the installation date. The length depends on what was purchased. Check your MCS certificate and your installation paperwork for the duration. If you are within the IBG period, you have a valid claim route.

How to claim: identify the insurance provider from your MCS certificate or installation contract, then contact them directly. You will normally need your original contract, the written guarantee, and any proof of payment. The insurer will assess your claim and, if it falls within the policy terms, appoint an authorised contractor to carry out the remedial work. You do not need to find the contractor yourself: that is the insurer's job.

If you cannot identify your insurance provider from your paperwork, contact RECC directly. They hold records of member insurance providers and may be able to trace the cover. If your installer was not RECC-registered and did not provide an IBG, which can happen with smaller or non-accredited companies, you will not have this route available. Move straight to the manufacturer warranty route and find a qualified local installer to carry out any remedial work.

What an IBG does not cover

An IBG covers workmanship faults from the original installation. It does not pay for annual servicing, inverter replacements caused by age rather than installation fault, cleaning, or general maintenance. For those needs, you need a qualified local repair company, not via an insurance claim. If your IBG period has expired, the same applies: find a company that will work on your existing system.

What to do if your system has a fault right now

If your system is generating less than it should, showing error codes, or has stopped producing entirely, you need a diagnosis. The good news is that any MCS-certified installer can legally work on a system they did not install. Certification is held by the engineer, not tied to the original installation.

The practical barrier is that not all companies will take this on. Some installers only work on their own installations, and some larger firms prioritise new builds over repairs. This is worth asking explicitly when you call: "Do you work on systems you did not install?" The ones that do will say yes without hesitation. The ones that do not will tell you, and that saves you time.

What a first diagnostic visit normally involves: reading the inverter error codes and any available monitoring data, carrying out a DC string test and isolation check, identifying whether the fault lies in the inverter, the panels, the wiring, or the monitoring system, and giving you a clear assessment of repair or replacement options with costs. We can often tell from the error code on the phone whether a site visit is definitely needed, or whether there is a firmware or reset step worth trying first.

Will my solar panels keep working if my installer goes out of business?

Yes. Solar panels generate electricity independently of the installer. Your system operates on your roof without any ongoing connection to the installation company, and there is nothing about the company dissolving that interrupts normal generation. Manufacturer warranties on the panels and inverter also survive installer closure. They are held by the equipment manufacturers and are not affected by the installer ceasing to trade. What is lost is the installer's own workmanship warranty, unless your installation included an Insurance Backed Guarantee.

What is an Insurance Backed Guarantee and do I have one?

An Insurance Backed Guarantee is an insurance policy that replaces the installer's workmanship warranty if they cease trading. It is a mandatory requirement for MCS-certified installers and RECC members, so if your system was installed by a certified company, one should have been issued. Check the top right corner of your MCS certificate. It will name the insurance provider and policy number if cover was registered. Will my Smart Export Guarantee payments continue if my installer goes bust?

Yes. Smart Export Guarantee payments are made by your energy supplier based on your smart meter readings. They are not dependent on the installation company in any way, and the company's dissolution has no effect on payments already in place. If you were not yet registered for SEG when your installer closed, you can still register. Most energy suppliers accept an MCS installation certificate as the basis for registration.
